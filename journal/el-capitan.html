900 metres of granite: the story of El Capitan

El Capitan rises about 900 metres from the floor of Yosemite Valley as one unbroken wall of granite.

El Capitan rises about 900 metres from the floor of Yosemite Valley in California. It is a single wall of granite, and from the valley it looks less like a mountain than a vertical cliff.

The valley itself was carved by glaciers. Over a very long time, moving ice ground down the landscape, leaving steep walls on either side and a wide, flat floor. El Capitan is one of the walls that was left standing.

That history is why the view is so striking. Because the granite is one unbroken face rather than a series of ledges and slopes, the eye has very little to rest on until it reaches the top.

If you are photographing it, look for something in the foreground, such as trees or the valley floor, to give the scale some context. On its own, a bare wall of rock can be hard to read.
